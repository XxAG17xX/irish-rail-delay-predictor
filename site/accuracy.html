These are scheduled samples, not visitor traffic. Almost nobody visits this site, so a scheduled job asks the live service a spread of questions every five minutes, one per lead band, sampled across whatever is in service rather than favouring any route. The rule fired, and nothing was patched

It was written in advance: if coverage on any line stays below 70% for a week, something has to be done. On 8 September it fired on the Cork corridor and the Kildare line, six weeks after the model was trained, while the DART and the Dublin hubs held. Those two corridors share track.

The ranges on those lines could have been widened until the number came back to 80%. That would have produced a better looking page and destroyed the evidence that the railway changed. So the number is published per line instead. If those corridors recover on their own, the shift was temporary, and that will be said here too.

Scored counts every prediction with a real arrival to check against. Matched counts the subset where the operator also published an estimate for the same train, station and moment, which is the only fair comparison.

By how far ahead you ask

Asking about the next stop is a different problem from asking about one an hour away, and the numbers should show that. They do: the error roughly triples across the range and coverage decays. A model whose ranges did not widen with the horizon would be the suspicious one.

On real delays over an hour, coverage is zero

Not low. Zero. It was zero for the previous model too, which appeared to cover a quarter of them until the labels behind that figure turned out to belong to other trains.

When a train loses seventy five minutes in one stretch, it was two to seven minutes late at every moment the service was asked. Nothing in "how late is it now, how far is left to go" can see that coming. The ranges are calibrated for ordinary lateness, and a wider one would mean being wrong about ordinary days to hedge against rare ones.

How often it answers at all

Accuracy without coverage is the misleading half of the story. A system that answers rarely and well is not the same product as one that answers often and well.

OutcomePredictionsShare

A prediction that could not be scored is reported here, not dropped. No arrival recorded means the operator never logged one for that stop. Arrival identical to the timetable is the signature of a scheduled time echoed back as though it were an observation, which is the data problem that shaped this whole project.
