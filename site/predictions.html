  • Timetable is the published arrival time. It never changes.
  • Irish Rail is the operator's own current estimate, the number you would read on the real departure board.
  • RailCast is this model's estimate, shown on flaps because it is the only number on the page this site is responsible for. Under it is the range the arrival should fall inside four times in five, and where the train last reported.

The board shows trains that have not arrived yet. Nothing here is a prediction made after the fact: every one is logged the moment it is made and marked the following morning.

A dash instead of a prediction

Many rows will show a dash under RailCast. That is the honest answer, not a failure: the model reads how late a train already is at the stops behind you, so a service that has not left its origin has reported nowhere and there is nothing to reason from.

Expect a lot of dashes at a terminus like Heuston, where nearly every service on the board is a departure that has not started, and far fewer at a through station like Kildare or Portarlington.

Those services are listed rather than hidden. Dropping them would make the coverage figure look better than the board you are actually standing in front of.
