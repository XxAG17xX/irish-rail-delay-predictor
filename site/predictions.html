Expected between 19:07 and 19:10 · last reported 5.0 min late at Park West and Cherry Orchard

  • A223 is Irish Rail's code for that one service on that one day. Train or DART says which network it is on: DART is the electric line along Dublin Bay, everything else is marked Train.
  • to Dublin Heuston is where the service is going, which is how you pick yours. Next to it, whether your station is where it starts, a stop it calls at, or where it terminates, then where it came from. Most services on a board only pass through, so your station usually will not appear in either name.
  • Timetable is the published time. It never changes.
  • Irish Rail is the operator's own estimate, the number on the real departure board.
  • RailCast is this site's estimate, on flaps. Under it, the range the arrival should fall inside four times in five, and where the train last reported.

Every service listed is still to arrive. Each RailCast time is saved the moment it is shown and checked against the real arrival the next morning.

A dash instead of a prediction

A dash under RailCast means there is no prediction for that service. RailCast works from how late a train already is at the stops behind you, so it can only answer once the train is moving and has reported somewhere.

You will see mostly dashes at a terminus like Heuston, where most services on the board start there, and far fewer at a through station like Kildare or Portarlington. Late in the evening most boards look like this, because the trains on them have not set off yet.

They stay on the board because they are still the trains you are waiting for. The times beside them are Irish Rail's.

Timetable is the published time. Irish Rail is the operator's own estimate. RailCast, on the flaps, is this site's, with the range the arrival should fall inside four times in five. A dash means RailCast has no prediction for that service, which is the case until the train is moving and has reported at a stop. How often the range is right is measured every night.

Boards are limited to a few per minute so the Irish Rail feed is not hammered. If you reach that limit the page says so and counts down.
