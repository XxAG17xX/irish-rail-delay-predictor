Five directions, derived from the railway rather than from the web

The first three I showed you were the three looks the official skill names as the AI clusters, so they are gone. These come from a different question: what does this subject look like as a physical object, and what did its world look like before the web? The railway has one of the richest graphic traditions of any industry, and almost none of it resembles a SaaS landing page.

The seven candidates I derived, in order of resonance:

  1. The signalling panel. Track as linework, state as lamp colour. A range is literally a section of track between two signals.
  2. The split flap departure board. The one railway interface everybody has read.
  3. The printed working timetable. A dense table of record with hairline rules and footnote daggers. Our accuracy page is a table of record.
  4. Station wayfinding. Flat saturated fields, signage grotesque, white keylines.
  5. The punctuality statistics bulletin. The operator's own published performance figures, plain and footnoted.
  6. The Edmondson ticket. Good as a motif, too small to carry a site.
  7. Ordnance Survey sheets. Beautiful, but contours have nothing to do with delay.

The first five are built below on the same real numbers. Look at the tables and the single service, not the headline, because that is where the reading actually happens.

1. Signal box

The interlocking panel a signaller reads. Slate ground, the journey drawn as track, and the 80% range shown as the section of track the train could be occupying when it arrives. Lamp colour carries state: green is meeting its promise, amber is not.

Risk: the metaphor has to survive four pages. If a chart cannot be drawn as track, the world stops halfway and becomes a dark theme with green accents.

2. Departure board

Solari split flap and the amber matrix above the platform. The predictions page stops being a web page and becomes the board itself, with a second line under each service carrying the range. The flap animation fires only when a number actually changes, which is the one authored motion moment on the site.

Risk: closest to costume of the five, and amber on black is punishing for long prose. The how it works page would need a quieter treatment, which means the world does not hold everywhere.

3. Working timetable

The printed working timetable: newsprint, condensed grotesque, hairline column rules, footnote daggers carrying the caveats. It treats the accuracy page as what it actually is, a table of record, and it gives the honest limitations a native home in the footnotes rather than in a coloured warning box.

Risk: brushes against the broadsheet cluster the skill warns about, and a paper ground on a phone in daylight is the one that most needs testing on your handset.

4. Signage

Station wayfinding. One saturated field owning the whole surface, a signage grotesque with highway lineage, white keylines, flat panels with no shadows anywhere. The most confident of the five and the one that would look least like a side project. Colour is a commitment rather than an accent.

Risk: a green this close to Iarnród Éireann's own could read as official. The field colour would have to move clearly away from theirs, and the site says it is unaffiliated on every page.

5. Statistics bulletin

The category standard, played straight and without irony: a punctuality statistics release. Numbered tables, footnotes, one flag colour, nothing decorative. If the argument of the whole project is honest measurement, this is the form that argument already has in the world. It is also the fastest to build and the easiest to keep readable on a phone.

Risk: it is the safe answer, and a reader who spends ninety seconds may remember nothing about it.

My pick is 1, Signal box, raised by 3. It is the only one where the visual world and the product's actual idea are the same thing: a range is a section of track, and lamp colour is exactly how a railway communicates confidence. It is dark, which suits a phone on a platform at night, and it will not be mistaken for any other portfolio site. From 3 it should take the footnote discipline, so the caveats live as daggers under the tables rather than in warning boxes.

If you want the memorable one instead, take 2 and accept a quieter variant for the long reading page. If you want the safe one, 5 is genuinely defensible and I will not argue you out of it. Say a number, or a number plus what you want taken from another.
